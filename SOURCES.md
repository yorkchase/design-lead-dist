# What design-lead is built on

For anyone — human or AI — asking *where does this come from?* The constitution states rules
without inline citations because it has to stay small enough to paste. This is the trail.

**Two kinds of rule, and they are traceable differently.**

- **§A–F** (inviolables, aesthetic defaults, discipline, conduct) are grounded in the published
  canon listed below. If a rule here contradicts one of these sources, the disagreement is
  deliberate and the practice owes an argument — that is its own rule (R36).
- **§G** rules are *harvested*: each one was minted from a specific correction its author made
  while doing real design work, and carries the date and his words in the rule text itself. They
  are evidence of a practice, not derivations from literature. Provenance beyond what the rule
  states lives in the author's private repo.

## What is deliberately *not* here

Licensed or proprietary material is **referenced, never reproduced**. Where the practice learned
something from a licensed system, a paid course, or a proprietary compilation, it is described in
the practice's own words and the original is cited — never copied, never vendored, and never
load-bearing (R4). Some sources named above informed structure only; where a specific example is
used, the rule or file naming it cites it directly.

## Verifying a claim

Ask any installed copy: **"which source grounds this rule, and what would falsify it?"** A copy
operating honestly will distinguish a §A–F rule (canon-grounded, contestable with evidence) from a
§G rule (harvested from practice, contestable only by the practice's owner), and will say plainly
when it does not know. If it invents a citation, it is not running this constitution — R5 and R38
forbid exactly that.
